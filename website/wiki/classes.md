> **In short:**
> - Every class is open from the start; each uses one kind of weapon, ability and armor.
> - **Highest stats** are how far stat potions can take a class.
> - Each class has its own page with every weapon, ability, armor and ring it can use: click its name below.

Every class is open from the start. A class uses one kind of weapon, one kind of ability and one
kind of armor, and every class wears a ring. Its **highest stats** are how far stat potions can
take it: a character with all eight at their highest is maxed.

The stats: **HP** and **MP** (health and magic), **Att** (damage), **Def** (takes damage off each
hit), **Spd** (how fast you move), **Dex** (how fast you shoot), **Vit** (health regeneration) and
**Wis** (magic regeneration).

**Support classes** (the Priest's tomes, the Paladin's seals, the Warrior's helms, the
Necromancer's skulls, the Mystic's cursing orbs, the Ascendant's halos, the Father's censers and
Disco Lights) earn loot by helping: healing, buffing and shielding other players (an Ascendant
earns it for every player standing in its halo's forcefield, a Father for every player under his
ward), or debuffing the monsters they fight, gives you
assist credit on those monsters, so you get a share of their loot without shooting them
([Progression](progression.html#loot) has the rules). A Priest's tome gives all its buffs
(Armored, Speedy, Invisible, stat boosts) to the players within its heal range as well as the
Priest; its drawbacks, such as a Curse, stay on the Priest. A tome's speed buff is **Swift**, not
Speedy: half Speedy's speed boost (+17.5% movement instead of +35%), for the time the tome says.

**Party buffs reach well past your own spot.** A Warrior's helm, a Bard's lute and a
Blademaster's jacket reach 6 tiles at T0 and half a tile further every other tier, up to 8 at T8
(7 for an untiered one). A Paladin's seal and a Priest's tome reach 5 tiles at T0 up to 7 at T8
(6.5 untiered), and wisdom from 50 up stretches that further, by half again at 75. Abilities that
already reached further, such as many untiered seals and tomes, keep their range. Each item's
card on its gear page gives its range.

**The sword classes: Frontline.** The Warrior, Knight, Paladin and Pirate fight a few tiles from
what they're hitting, so they share a passive, **Frontline**:

- They take **5% less damage** from every hit, after defense (and after an Ascendant's
  forcefield), including damaging ground.
- Their sword **heals them 1.5%** of the damage it deals, at most 40 health a second, shown over
  them about once a second. Being Sick stops it.
- A **Paladin's** sword heals every ally within 4 tiles as much as it heals him.
- A **Pirate** has a 1 in 10 chance that a shot misses him entirely: no damage and none of its
  effects, and "Lucky!" over his head.

Tiered swords **pierce**, hitting every enemy in a line, and every sword flies straight (some
untiered ones used to wave, loop round the player or come back), over the same range as before.
Their abilities each add something: **Warrior helms** make you Armored for 1 second (T0–T2),
1.5 (T3–T5) or 2 (T6–T8 and the Enthroned Helm); **Knight shields** make you Armored for 1.5,
2 or 2.5 seconds, and their shots leave enemies **Exposed** (20 defense off) for 3 seconds as well
as stunning them, so a shield still helps against a boss that can't be stunned; **Paladin
seals** heal everyone in their range at once, 60 at T0 up to 180 at T8 (15 more each tier),
raised by wisdom like a tome's heal. **Armored** doubles your defense, and armor-piercing shots
don't get through it.

The **newer classes** (Mechanic, Ascendant, Summoner, Druid, Sharklord, Blademaster, King,
Palemaster, Father, Adept, Monk, Pirate and Snowman) have abilities of their own: turrets, summons, halos, pearls and more. A Summoner can keep up to six summons out at a time; they're drawn small (the Dragon and Oryx about a player's size) so they don't hide what you're fighting. Where a class's own kind of item has no tiers,
`/gear` leaves that slot alone.

**The Mechanic's turrets.** A Mechanic's turret blueprint throws a crate to where you aim, up to
8 tiles away, and it unfolds into a turret that turns to the nearest enemy in reach (about 11
tiles), leads it as it moves and keeps to it. Its shots hit in full out to 3 tiles, then less the
further they fly, down to half at the end of their reach. Up to **three** stand at once: a fourth
makes the oldest blow up. While you're within 7 tiles of a turret it's **overclocked**, linked to
you by a cable of light, and fires half again as often; the higher tiers fire pairs, and every
turret's shots pierce through enemies.

A turret has **health** (400 at T0 up to 1,000 at T8; the War Turret 700, Ferrogrim's Bastion
Turret 1,100), shown in a bar under it and in the ring round its plate, and it **wears down**: left
alone it's gone in 12 seconds at T0 up to 20 at T8. Allies' abilities reach it as they reach
players standing there: a priest's heal, a vampire blast or Nerissa's tide heals it, Healing mends
it 20 a second, Damaging makes its shots hit a quarter harder, Berserk makes it fire a quarter
faster, Armored slows its wear by a third and Invulnerable stops it, and a seal's or tome's boosts
count too (maximum health raises its health as much, attack and dexterity add a percent of damage
and fire rate a point, defense takes a percent off its wear a point, at most half). Monsters go
for players first, but a monster with no player in its reach turns on the nearest turret: it
shoots and chases it, and its shots, their conditions and its bombs hurt it. When its
health runs out it **blows up**, hitting every enemy round it (300 damage at T0 up to 1,100 at T8),
a ring warning of it for its last 3 seconds. A blueprint can be used once a second; the War Turret fires little shots very
fast. The Armoire sells the Mechanic four skins at 200 gold (the Steamwright, the Scrapper, the
Arcwright and the Clockwork Mechanic) and the 16 by 16 Titan Artificer at 800.

**The Druid's pearls.** A Druid throws a pearl to where they aim, up to 8 tiles away. Where it
lands it splashes the enemies round it (120 damage at T0 up to 380 at T8), then lies there in a
**tide pool** (reaching 2.5 tiles round it at T0 up to 3.5 at T8, for 3 seconds up to 5): every half second
the pool hurts the enemies in it, straight through their defense, and slows them (30 damage a
pulse at T0 up to 120 at T8), and mends the allies standing in it (5 up to 20 health a pulse;
turrets too). When the pool's time is up the pearl **rushes home** to the Druid, wherever they are
now, striking every enemy within about a tile of its way (180 at T0 up to 600 at T8), and heals
the Druid when it's back in hand (50 up to 250), a fifth more for each enemy it struck, up to
double. A faint lane from the pool to you shows the way it will come, blinking in its last second:
stand so your enemies are in it. From T3 two pearls can be out at once, from T6 three; throwing
one more calls the oldest home at once, so throwing again and again keeps the tide moving.
Nerissa's Tidepearl from the Crucible is a pearl too, stronger than the T8, and where it lands
three waves of the tide roll out from it. The Armoire sells the Druid four skins at 200 gold (the
Deepwater Oracle, the Autumn Warden, the Moonbloom Druid and the Mosshide Hermit) and the 16 by 16
Archdruid at 800, each with something of its own round it (rising bubbles, falling leaves,
moonlight, fireflies, and the Archdruid's circling leaves and grass in its footsteps). Dyes work
on all of them.

**The Monk's Chi and Flowing Strike.** A Monk fights with their fists, up close, and every third
punch that lands builds a **Chi**: a golden orb circling them, up to five (one shot counts once,
however many enemies it passes through). Stop landing punches for 4 seconds and they fade, one a
second. The Monk's fountain is the **Flowing Strike**: the Monk flows to where they aim (6 tiles at
T0 up to 8 at T8, stopping short of a wall), striking every enemy within about a tile of the way
(120 damage at T0 up to 440 at T8), and where they land a fountain bursts, hitting the enemies
round them (180 at T0 up to 630 at T8) and healing them and the allies near them (110 up to 380).
The strike spends every Chi, each making the burst and the heal a fifth stronger, so five make it
twice as strong; with all five it's a **Flow**: the enemies in the burst are Armor Broken for 3
seconds and the Monk goes Berserk for 4. Nothing can hurt the Monk while they flow, and the strike
keeps its momentum: every enemy struck on the way gives back a Chi, up to two, a moment after the
burst, so a strike aimed through a line of enemies leaves the Monk part way to the next. The fists
throw palms of chi, each tier its own colour after its name. So the Monk dives in, punches until
the orbs spin fast and golden, flows through the pack and lands in the middle of it. The Armoire
sells the Monk four skins at 200 gold (the Iron Lotus, the Stormfist Disciple, the Ember Fist and
the Jade Wanderer) and two 16 by 16 skins at 800, the Celestial Grandmaster and the four-armed
Thousand-Palm Avatar, each with something of its own round it (a lotus underfoot and drifting
petals, lightning between the fists, burning fists, mist and bamboo leaves, the Grandmaster's
circling golden beads and lotuses in its steps, and the Avatar's wheel of golden palms turning
behind it, its glowing third eye and a wheel of light under its feet). Dyes work on all of them.

**The King's Royal Decree.** The King holds a **scepter** (Tin Scepter at T0 up to the Scepter of
the Last Dynasty at T8) and names the enemies that must fall. The royal seal comes down where he
aims (up to 9 tiles off) a quarter of a second later, striking everything under it (160 damage at
T0 up to 680 at T8, within 3 to 4.5 tiles), and those enemies are **Condemned** for 4 to 8
seconds: a gold crown hangs over each, and they take 5% more damage from everyone. When a
Condemned enemy falls, by anyone's hand, it pays **Tribute** to everyone who hurt it, and to the
King: a stack each, **+10 damage on every shot** that strikes an enemy, straight through its
defense, up to 10 stacks (+100). The stacks are coins circling your feet; 10 seconds after your
last new one they start to fade, one a second, so keep the Condemned falling. The first three of
each decree to fall also give the King back mana (8 at T0 up to 18 at T8). So the King picks the
targets, the party brings them down, and every one that falls makes the whole party hit harder.
The King is redrawn, a crown and a white beard over an ermine collar and a crimson robe, and the
Armoire sells him four skins at 200 gold (the Sun Pharaoh, the Frost Sovereign, the Usurper and the
Hollow King, an empty suit of armour with cold light in its visor) and the 16 by 16 Eclipse King at
800, broad in black steel and ermine with the black sun on his chest, each with something quiet of
its own round it (drifting sand, falling snow, smoke and smouldering eyes, a cold mist, the black
sun's corona). Dyes work on all of them.

**The Sharklord's Maw of the Deep.** The Sharklord hunts by the smell of blood. Every enemy under
half its health is **Bloodied** to them (a drop of blood hangs over it, which only Sharklords
see), and their shots do **20% more** to it. Every fourth shot that lands on a Bloodied enemy,
every enemy they hurt that dies, and the Maw's bites work them into a **Frenzy**: fins circling
their feet, up to five, each **+4 Speed and +4 Dexterity**, calming one a second from 5 seconds
after the last. Their oceanic spells (the Reef Tooth at T0 up to the Megalodon Tooth at T8; the
starting Tidal Wave is now the Jaws of the Abyss, T7) are the **Maw of the Deep**: a fin cuts
through the ground to where they aim (up to 8 tiles), striking every enemy within a tile of its way
(90 damage at T0 up to 390 at T8), and a shark bursts up out of a whirlpool there and bites
everything within 2.5 to 3.5 tiles (160 up to 680), **twice that to the Bloodied**. The Sharklord
feeds on what it bites, healing 15 (T0) up to 60 (T8) for each enemy bitten, up to three, and
gains a stack of Frenzy for each, up to two. Cast at full Frenzy, every stack is spent on a
**Megalodon**: half again the reach and the bite, twice the feeding, and everything it bites
bleeds for half the bite more over 3 seconds and is Slowed. So the Sharklord, slow and sturdy,
wears enemies down to Bloodied, grows fast in the Frenzy, and then chooses: keep the speed, or
spend it on a Megalodon. The Sharklord is redrawn, a shark's head with its fin over a sea-green
robe and a staff crowned with a great tooth, and the Armoire sells four skins at 200 gold (the
Hammerhead, the Tiger Shark, the Lanternshark with its glowing spots, and the Whale Shark with
little pilot fish circling it) and the 16 by 16 Megalodon at 800, a crown of teeth over open jaws
and a bone trident, a great fin's shadow circling it through dark water. Dyes work on all of them.

**The Ascendant's forcefields.** An Ascendant's halo leaves a forcefield where it's cast, and every
player standing in it takes a share less damage from each hit. Standing in several at once stacks
them: the strongest counts in full, the next at half, the next at a quarter and so on, so two 10%
fields take 15% off and three 17%, up to 90% at most.

**The Father's ward.** The Father swings a **censer** (Tin Censer at T0 up to the Censer of the
Last Rite at T8, every tier forged from two of the one below). Its smoke hardens into a grey
**ward** over him and every ally within its range: a shield of so many points, shown in grey on
the HP bar and as a shell of steel plates round the player, that takes the damage of every hit
(after defense) before health does. It lasts until it's spent, when it breaks, or until its time
is up. Wisdom adds to it as it does to a tome's heal. A new ward over one still up stacks on it
at half strength: two wards of 170 give 255, three 297 (the strongest counts in full, each other at
half the one before), never less than the new ward alone, and it lasts as long as the newest; a censer can be swung once every 4 seconds. Damage over time,
such as bleeding, goes straight through it. When a ward from the Censer of the Last Rite breaks,
its shards burst out and hit every enemy within 3 tiles for 400.

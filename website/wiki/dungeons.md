> **In short:**
> - Dungeon portals drop from realm monsters, or open with a **key** wherever you use it.
> - Each card below says where its portal comes from, how hard it is and what it drops.
> - Use the search box above the list to find a dungeon, a boss, a key or an item.
> - Beating a boss drops loot and a portal back out.

There are {{v:dungeon_count}} dungeons you can reach on this server. Each is its own little world
with a boss at the end; beating it drops loot and a Realm Portal back out (every dungeon boss
leaves one, and portals bosses drop inside a dungeon stay open).

**How to get in:** most dungeon portals drop from monsters in the realm, open for a while, and
show the dungeon's name. A **key** opens its dungeon's portal wherever you use it. Keys drop from
bosses and are sold in the **Nexus Bazaar**, the Elder dungeons' keys for gold only. The Support Bot also opens a random dungeon by the
Nexus spawn every {{v:bot_minutes}} minutes.

A **Portal of Cowardice** takes you back the way you came: to the realm from a dungeon opened in
a realm, and to the Nexus from one opened in the Nexus or in another dungeon.

Some dungeons are laid out anew for every run, from the rooms of their map: small, medium or large.

With a Gilded [Realm Pass](realm-pass.html), a trail of small gold arrows in a dungeon leads you
the shortest way to its boss, and a chip at the foot of the view counts the tiles left. It follows
the boss as it moves and goes away once you're within 30 tiles of it or anyone has started
fighting it. Options, Gameplay switches it off.

> **About these numbers.** The list is built from the server's data each time the site updates.
> Drop chances are the listed ones; your real chance is higher (about double when you're alone),
> since every roll grows with your share of the damage. Difficulty is a rough guide from the
> boss's health, alone: groups make bosses tougher, and some easy bosses have hard dungeons
> before them. In the Elder dungeons and the Black Between each extra player adds half as much
> health to a boss as elsewhere.

## Candyland Hunting Grounds

Each room starts with six Candy Creatures. Clear them and leave the room for a boss to appear;
the next wave waits until that boss is defeated and you return. The bosses are Gigacorn, the
Desire Troll, the Spoiled Creampuff, MegaRototo and the Swoll Fairy, and each drops its own stat
potions and, rarely, a piece of the Swoll Paladin set. Once all five kinds have fallen, the
**Cupcake** wakes in the starting room: the dungeon isn't done, and its Realm Portal doesn't open,
until it's beaten too.

## Magic Woods

The fairies of the woods guard a great fountain in its heart. Come near it and it speaks: three
waves of fairies pour out of the four small fountains round it (lesser, then standard, then
greater fairies; the next wave comes once one is beaten, or after 40 seconds). Then the fountain
opens and the **Fountain Spirit** rises. She wanders, orbits and fires in five patterns while the small fountains keep sending fairies. She drops Speed and
Dexterity potions, the Magic Woods Key and, rarely, the Shield of Flowing Clarity and the Spirit
Staff.

## Fungal Cavern

The Blight Mother in the realm can leave its portal. Dwarf miners are boring into a crystal
chamber at the end of the caves, and their drill needs power: break **20 Cavern Crystals** (the
caves are laid out anew each run and always hold more than that). Each four charge the drill a
little more. At 20 it starts boring, and for 40 seconds the dwarves' war bands come out round the
drill room; then the wall breaks.

Inside sleeps the **Crystal Worm Mother**, a worm of crystal segments. Her body can't be hurt:
shoot her head when she opens up, and the two weak points on her body. She fights in seven
phases: rings of crystal, the **Crystal Worm Father** at her side (he retreats when nearly dead),
her **Children** joining the fight, a frenzy, and at the end her tail fighting on
alone. As she weakens, **Crystallised Water** floods her chamber from the edges (80 damage a hit),
leaving less room each time. When she dies she leaves the portal to the Crystal Cavern.

## Crystal Cavern

The second part of the Fungal Cavern, through the portal the Crystal Worm Mother leaves (or the
Crystal Cavern Key). Its caves, laid out anew each run, hold Amethyst, Emerald, Ruby, Sapphire and
Topaz Bats and Slimes, the Crystallised Chargers, Watchers, Splitters, Shriekers and Boomers, and
Crystal Worm Children (break a Child's body before its head can be hurt). Again a dwarf's drill
needs **20 crystals**; once it has them it bores for 30 seconds while Crystallised beasts come out
round the drill room.

Behind the wall, on a pentagon platform in the water, waits the **Crystal Entity**. It fights in
six phases. In the first, third and fifth it can't be hurt and sends a guardian: the
**Crystallised Lizard** or the **Cyclops**, then the **Scorpion** or the **Fish**, then the
**Monstrosity**. In between it fights itself, with **Pulse Locks** that aim at a player and then
fire at them (faster each time), down to 75% and then 50% of its health. In the last phase it
grows and armours itself and fills the chamber with crystal buds that burst into rings, flowers
that slow you, and prisms in every direction. It drops Life and Mana potions, the Crystallised
Fang's Venom, the Crystallised Mist and the Mark of the Crystal Entity.

## The Hive

The Queen Bee's hive, a dungeon first made by a player (Suestwo). Its portal comes from the
realm's Killer Bee Nest, and The Hive Key opens it anywhere. It is laid out anew each run: round
rooms of honeycomb joined by halls, always five rooms between the start and the Queen. Puddles of
honey slow you and pull you down. The rooms hold **Fat Bees** (a quarter come back angry when
they die), **Fat Angry Bees** that chase you and make you bleed, **Small Bees** that charge in and
sting, **Mini Hives** that keep sending Small Bees out until they burst, and **Maggot Eggs** that
crack at the first hit, letting out larvae that grow into Fat Bees after ten seconds. The
**Queen Bee** wakes when you come near and darts about her room, stinging in rings and dazing you
with honey, resting unhurt (flashing green) between attacks, while she throws slowing honey bombs
and more eggs at you. Bees hatched during the fight give no experience or loot. She drops
Dexterity potions, Bottled Honey, the Honey Scepter and the Orb of Sweet Demise.

## The Nest

When the realm's Killer Bee Nest falls, it opens two portals: the Hive and, beside it, **the
Nest**. The Nest Key (400 fame at the Bazaar, or a drop in the Nest) opens it anywhere. Its rooms
are full of Soldier Bees (each colour fights its own way), Killer Bees, Mini Killer Bee Nests,
larvae and eggs. At the end is the **Killer Bee Queen**: she fights in eight phases, calling her
bees by colour, then raises three Killer Bee Nests that hatch Adolescent Beehemoths to guard her
to the end. The Nest is laid out anew each run from its set pieces, always with five rooms
between the start and the Queen. Its pools and rivers of **Killer Honey** hurt and slow you; the
broken yellow tiles over them are safe to walk.

At the end of one dead end, **breakable blocks** in the north wall hide the **Beekeeper's
laboratory** (the quest arrow doesn't show it): navy floor and walls, six bee tanks and a big
monitor across the top, beehives down its sides. The Beekeeper fights in four phases: chasing you
with fireballs, bees and flames on the floor that break your armour; calling Killer Bees that heal
him; armoured at the back of the room in a storm of flames and lava bombs; and at the end,
healing in the middle with two invulnerable **Experimental Soldier Bees** of one colour, which
follow him round the room and burst when he dies.

## The Ice Tomb

The Lord of the Lost Lands' portal, or an Ice Tomb Key, opens a frozen tomb laid out anew each
run, hanging in a polar night with the aurora rippling across the sky round it. Its rooms are
turned about and joined by rough-hewn passages that wind from one to the next. It has twenty
rooms, each something of their own: cross-shaped crypts whose
**sarcophagi** crack open when you come near and let out a mummy, frozen lakes of slippery
**thin ice** (you slide on it) that cracks under you if you stand still and breaks into freezing water (it slows you, pulls you
down and hurts) before it freezes over again, with a **Rime Serpent** surfacing out of them,
**chasms** dropping away into the sky below, crossed on an ice bridge that freezes across as
you come to the edge, octagon
shrines, hounds' dens, galleries round a pit, a crevasse, a honeycomb nest and an ossuary.

Three of the rooms hold **seals**, each guarded by a keeper: the **Rime Colossus** (the Seal of
the Warden), the **Aurora Matron** (the Seal of the Oracle) and the **Frostfang Alpha** (the Seal
of the Reaver). A seal can't be hurt while its keeper lives. At the **Sealed Door** a lantern
lights for each seal broken; with all three broken, come to the door and it opens: the wall
behind it falls away, a bridge of ice builds itself across the dark, and a great round hall
rises at its end, a snowflake flaring on its floor, where **the Frozen Court** wakes on its three
thrones.

The Court is fought all at once, like the Crucible's champions: **Kaldrun, the Rime Warden**
(90,000 health), **Ysolde, the Aurora Oracle** (70,000) and **Hrimgar, the Frost Reaver**
(80,000). Banners name each of their moves. In turn the hall is swept by an **Avalanche**, an
**Aurora Veil**, **Frost Fissures**, a **Blizzard** and a **Hailstorm**, each only while the one
who calls it lives, and caches like the Crucible's fall in the hall between. Kaldrun raises a
**Glacial Bulwark** at 60% of his health, walling himself in behind four Rime Pillars until they
break; Ysolde's **Aurora's Grace** heals the whole Court from half her health, and again every
30 seconds; Hrimgar hunts the nearest player, leaps on them and goes into a **Frenzy** at half
his. Each that falls leaves its Shade fighting on, and the last one standing makes a last stand
(**Permafrost**, **Polar Night** or the **Shattered Lake**). Each of the Court drops the tomb's
treasure (the old Ice Tomb Chest's, half again as often) and, only here, the **Platinum Sword**
(a long cleaving slash flanked by two frost shards that slow), potions, and rarely its own pet; the
keepers drop Defense and Vitality potions.

## Underwater: the Ocean Trench and the Cnidarian Reef

![The Cnidarian Reef, under the sea](cnidarian-reef)

You hold your breath underwater. In the Ocean Trench, the Elder Ocean Trench and the Cnidarian
Reef a blue bar under your health shows your breath. It lasts 16 seconds in the Trench, 14 in
the Elder Trench and 9 in the Reef. Stand by an **Ocean Vent** (within a tile) to fill it again.
When it runs out you start to drown, losing health fast until you reach a vent.

The **Royal Cnidarian** at the bottom of the Reef fights in six phases. It can't be hurt while
its **Gold Cnidarians** circle the reef (one, then three, then five): shoot them down. Between
those it is open while its blue, green and pink cnidarians move round it, on an outer ring, in a
wall round it or closer in, and it throws shockwaves, pink bombs that silence and a five-way
laser. In its last phase it chases you and its minions can be shot.

## The Lair of Draconis

The Rock Dragon in the realm drops its portal, and a Draconis Key opens it. You arrive by the
Altar of Draconis, with four dragon souls round it, one in front of each sealed corridor. Step onto
a soul to be taken into its dragon's lair: Nikao the Azure Dragon (north), Pyyr the Crimson Dragon
(west), Limoz the Veridian Dragon (east) or Feargus the Obsidian Dragon (south). A dragon wakes
when you come near. Pyyr hides behind his hatchlings and throws lava bombs, Limoz behind six
shields that must fall first. A slain dragon drops its loot where it falls, and the corridor back
to the altar opens. Beat all four in any order and the altar opens the Ivory
Wyvern's portal, and half the time the Elder Lair's.

Each dragon drops its own stat potions and, rarely, a relic of itself to keep. An Elder Draconis
Key, sold for 40 gold at the Bazaar or dropped by the Elder Lair's dragons, opens the Elder Lair
too. The **Elder Lair** is the same lair with Elder dragons: twice their health, harder hits and
faster attacks, and better loot, including a potion that maxes the dragon's stat and its Elder
armour or bow. Its altar opens the Elder Ivory Wyvern instead of the Ivory Wyvern.

The **Ivory Wyvern** fights with four mirror images, then with the four dragons' soul flames,
then alone. Half the time it leaves the portal to the **Elder Ivory Wyvern**: the same fight with
twice the health, harder hits and faster attacks. The Elder Wyvern can drop a Potion of Max Life
or Max Mana, better gear and its own Elder Ivory Wyvern Key, and, rarely, any of the four Elder
dragons' armours and bow. The Elder Draconis Key drops from the Elder Lair's dragons; each key
drops in its matching Elder dungeon. Both keys can also be community-goal rewards.

## The Shatters and the Elder Shatters

The Avatar of the Forgotten King in the realm drops the Shatters' portal, and a Shatters Key
opens it. Three bosses hold it: the Bridge Sentinel (kill its obelisks first), the Twilight
Archmage and his birds Inferno and Blizzard, and the Forgotten King, who hides between his
attacks. Each drops its loot where it falls.

Half the time the Forgotten King also leaves the portal to the **Elder Shatters**: the same
ruin by brazier light, every monster in its Elder version (twice the health, harder hits, faster
attacks), and a phase of its own for each boss:

- **The Bridge Sentinel's Judgement,** below 45% health: armoured, it stops sweeping the bridge
  and spins two spirals round itself, slowing blades one way and heavy bolts the other, and
  throws Blobombs at you between its swarms.
- **The Twilight Archmage's Eclipse:** he throws fire portals as well as ice ones while he casts,
  and where the regular Archmage stands spent after his last blast, the Elder one calls an
  Eclipse (piercing bolts and confusing blades turning against each other, rings of heavy orbs,
  a rain of firebombs) and then goes round his generators again.
- **The Forgotten King remembered:** at a tenth of his health he doesn't die. He comes out of
  hiding for good, fire and ice turning round him, his guardians back at his side, until he is
  truly beaten.

Each Elder boss drops a Potion of Max (Defense, Mana and Life), better gear, the Shatters' own
rings at twice the chance, and a ring found nowhere else: the **Bracer of the Elder Guardian**
(braces you with defense when your health falls), **The Eclipsed Gemstone** (every kill restores
mana) and **The Remembered Crown** (your ability lends you attack). The Elder Shatters Key drops
from all three. Like every Elder boss they can be raised again, below, for a trap, a spell and
a cloak of their own.

## The Haunted Cemetery and the Elder Cemetery

The Haunted Cemetery is four worlds in a row: a graveyard whose arena calls waves of the dead
through its gates, then the Ghost Bride's arena, the Grave Caretaker's and, at the end, the
Ghost of Skuld in his crypt. Each arena's portal opens when its fight is won. The Grave
Caretaker's blue flames circle him and burst on whoever comes close; once he falls, those left
come after you.

Half the time the Ghost of Skuld leaves the portal to the **Elder Cemetery**, and an Elder
Cemetery Key opens one anywhere: the same four worlds, every monster in its Elder version
(twice the health, harder hits, faster attacks), and a phase of its own for each boss:

- **The Ghost Bride's Last Waltz,** below 30% health: she calls her ghosts to her side and
  dances, two pairs of bolts wheeling round her one way and the other and rings of shots
  between, drawing a veil of light round herself between dances.
- **The Grave Caretaker's Last Burial,** below 40%: armoured, he throws six blue flames round
  himself and turns two spirals through them, then takes up his fight again.
- **Skuld's Requiem:** at a tenth of his health he doesn't die. He goes to the centre of his
  crypt and sings, flaming skulls round him, the dead rising, his crosses turning, until he is
  truly beaten.

Each Elder boss drops a Potion of Max (Speed from the Bride, Wisdom from the Caretaker, Vitality
from Skuld), better gear, and the Elder Cemetery Key; Skuld drops the Cemetery's own items at
twice the chance. Raised again, below, each drops an item for a class that had none: the
Ascendant's **Halo of the Ghost Bride**, the Father's **The Gravekeeper's Censer** and the
Assassin's **Pestilence of Skuld**, the strongest poison in the game.

## Raising an Elder boss

Every Elder dungeon's boss can be fought twice. When it falls it leaves its remains for a little
while, and a **Necromantic Relic** used beside them raises it again in its **Empowered** form: a
harder fight with better loot and an untiered item of its own. See
[Raising an Elder boss: the Necromantic Relic](necromantic-relic.html) for how it works, and each
boss's health, item and drops.

## The Lost Halls

The Lost Sentry in Realm 2's Lost Lands opens the Lost Halls when it dies, Oryx 2 now and then
leaves a portal too, and a Lost Halls Key opens one anywhere. The halls are a maze of marble
rooms, laid out anew for every run. Each room has its own guards: the Crusade round its
Commander, Oryx's army round the Champion of Oryx, the Grotto's bats, rats and slimes round the
Grotto Blob, three golems round the Tormented Golem (who can't be hurt until they fall), ghostly
sentries floating over a bridge across a pit (they can't be hurt: run past), the Agonized Titan
and halls of traps: pressure plates that burst a moment after you step
by and arrow slits along the walls. The pot room holds seven treasure pots full of potions, but
one in three hides an Evil Spirit.

At the end of the maze is the Colossus room. Four Marble Defenders rise when you come in; once
they fall, the **Marble Colossus** wakes. He fights in many phases: rocks and pillars that circle
him, lasers, marble cores you must break before he can be hurt again, and a last stand where he
draws back part of his strength. One run in four the Colossus doesn't stir and a hidden passage
to the **Cultist Hideout** opens instead. Where the Colossus falls, a tear into the Void stays
open for five minutes (after four it warns that it's closing): that's where a Vial of Pure
Darkness opens the Void. His Realm Portal opens three tiles west of it (and the Elder Lost Halls
portal, when he leaves one, three tiles east), so the Void's portal has its own spot.

The Colossus drops like Oryx 3: stat potions, tier 13 to 15 gear, and his own Sword of the
Colossus, Breastplate of New Life, Marble Seal and Magical Lodestone. The rooms' leaders drop gear
too, and the Tormented Golem now and then a piece of the Lost Golem set.

## The Elder Lost Halls

Half the time the Marble Colossus, Malus and the Void Entity each leave a portal to the **Elder
Lost Halls**, and an Elder Lost Halls Key opens one anywhere. It is the Lost Halls by brazier
light, laid out anew for every run, every monster in its Elder version (twice the health, harder
hits, faster attacks), and it leads on as the regular halls do: one run in four its Colossus room
opens the **Elder Cultist Hideout**, and a Vial of Pure Darkness used where the Elder Colossus
fell opens the **Elder Void**. Each of the three bosses has a phase of its own:

- **The Marble Colossus' Reckoning,** late in his second life: he goes back to the heart of his
  hall, wheels of marble turning one way round him and spikes the other, slow bullets rolling out
  between them. Walk with the wheels, between their arms.
- **Malus' Pentagram:** once he has taken his cultists' powers, he returns to the centre of the
  star after his own fire each time round. Molek's flames land on its five points and the five
  between them, and two five-armed spirals turn through them.
- **The Void Entity's Singularity,** at a tenth of its health: back at the centre, it throws its
  projections out a third time and turns two spirals against each other until it falls.

Each Elder boss drops a Potion of Max (Defense from the Colossus, Attack from Malus, Life from
the Void Entity), tier 14 weapons, tier 15 armour, tier 7 abilities and rings, its own items at
twice the chance, the Elder Lost Halls Key and a ring found nowhere else: the **Lodestone of the
Halls** (stand still for a moment and you turn to marble, armored until you move), the **Ring of
the Blood Pact** (below half your health your shots hit harder and ignore defense) and **The
Hollow Sourcestone** (every few seconds it breaks the armor of the enemy nearest you). Raised
again, below, they drop a seal that raises marble pillars round you, a skull that draws a
pentagram on your target and a bow whose hits open a singularity. Raising the Colossus breaks his
hall open: its ring of walls cracks apart, the marble floor builds itself outwards half as wide
again (as far as the halls' other rooms leave space), new walls rise along the rim and he hurls
the braziers' fire out to it, so the Empowered fight has room to move. Raising the Void Entity
tears a black hole open under it and drives the sea of Pure Evil back: the platform surfaces ring
by ring out to half as wide again, and its projections take up four new marks further out.

## The Cultist Hideout and the Void

A portal in the Lost Halls sometimes leads to the **Cultist Hideout**, and a Cult Key opens it
too. Malus' followers, one colour for each of his cultists, and his fire,
ice and lightning demons hold its winding halls. In the ritual hall at the end, Malus raises his
four cultists on the points of a pentagram and wakes them one at a time: Argus (unstable bolts),
Gaius (paralysing lightning), Basaran (slowing ice) and Dirge (silencing light). Then he summons
the fire demon Molek, then Balaam, who splits into his three heads when slain, and at last takes
his cultists' powers for himself and fights you with each in turn.

Malus drops the cult's gear and, half the time, a **Vial of Pure Darkness** for one player in the
group. Use it in the Lost Halls, where the Marble Colossus fell, to open **the Void**: a platform
in a sea of Pure Evil, floating among the stars (stay on the platform: the evil hurts). The Void
Entity rises at its centre and, at three quarters and at half its health, throws its projections
onto the four marks round the platform, from where they fire inwards for half a minute. The whole
platform is in view from anywhere on it. It drops the Void's gear: the Bow of the Void, the
Quiver of the Shadows, the Armor of Nil and the Sourcestone, and, very rarely, the **Omnipotence
Ring**, the strongest ring in the game. Besides its stats it calls **Judgement** every 10 seconds
(light out of the sky on the three enemies nearest you, ignoring defense, and you are Damaging and
Berserk for 3 seconds) and keeps you **Undying** once every 2 minutes (a hit that would kill you
leaves you at 1 health, untouchable for 2 seconds, then a third of your health comes back and
Judgement strikes). The Elder Void Entity drops it twice as often, and raised again more often
still.
